# Sound and music editing

Sound often decides whether a cut feels professional. Dialogue first, then the world around it.

## Build the sound world (separate tracks/buses)
Dialogue · narration/VO · room tone & ambience · production effects · Foley · hard effects · designed effects ·
music · archival. Establish *space* before decoration: a room-tone bed makes an aggressive dialogue edit invisible;
a door, footstep, cloth move or vehicle can join two shots better than a visual transition. Hold a spotting pass:
note tone, effects, dialogue problems, ADR needs.

## Sound as an editing tool
J/L cuts let sound lead or trail picture; ambience changes can signal a new location before the image does; silence
is a choice; a sound can motivate a smash cut. Check cuts with picture off.

## Music
1. Find the cue's **emotional purpose** before cutting to its beat — support point of view and structure, don't
   announce every emotion.
2. Mark phrases, downbeats, changes and endings; cut/extend at **phrase boundaries**; use fades and crossfades.
   Resolve 20's AI Music Editor (Studio) can lengthen/shorten beat-driven music by musical edits rather than
   stretching — audition for artifacts.
3. **Duck** under dialogue (track automation or bus processing); remove music when silence is stronger.
4. Keep stems or clearly labelled versions; verify licensing and cue information. Temp music shapes taste — note it.

## Before the mix
Clean track naming/colour, room tone under edits, clip gain roughly balanced, buses for DX/MX/FX, stems planned
(`resolve-guide-fairlight`, `resolve-delivery-specs` for loudness). Listen on headphones, speakers and in mono.

Related: `editing-principles` (rhythm), `resolve-audio` (MCP-driven audio planning).
