# Wedding delivery and client care

A wedding film holds private moments, other people's faces and music someone owns. Practical guidance from published studio practice, library terms and standards pages (the Sotto DaVinci Resolve knowledge base (Notion) › Vertical: Wedding), dated 2026-11-22.
**Not legal advice**; privacy, recording-consent, copyright and contract law vary by jurisdiction, so say so and point the user to a local lawyer for contract wording. Verify every licence and platform term before delivery.

## Before the shoot (terms)
- **Deliverables as ranges**, turnaround per deliverable (published norms: teaser in about 1–2 weeks, full package 6–12 weeks, season-dependent), when the clock starts, peak extension, rush option.
- **Revisions**: one or two consolidated rounds with timestamped notes; define minor (trims, title fixes, level or colour tweaks) vs major re-edit; music replacement; extra-round fee; deemed approval.
- **Venue and officiant rules** in writing first (camera positions, aisle movement, flash/LED/drone, microphones, publication). They override the couple's wishes and are a disclosed limit on shots and audio.
- **Rights**: who owns raw footage, project files and edits; client licence (personal sharing and posting); studio portfolio licence with an opt-out; no resale or re-editing; raw footage terms.
- **Sensitive-moments protocol**: ask about no-film people, conflict, medical issues, children, private or religious rituals, surprises; keep a do-not-film list for every shooter and editor.

## Music
1. Music on film needs a **sync licence and a master licence**. A venue's or DJ's performance licence covers neither; buying or streaming a song, crediting it or keeping the film private does not either.
2. Use a library or commissioned music whose licence names client work and the platforms (plan tiers differ: some cover only the creator's own channels, wedding single-song licences may exclude business promotion).
3. Save invoice, licence certificate, track, plan, download date, project and platforms with the project. "Royalty-free" is not "unrestricted".
4. Platform music is cleared for that platform's own use only; Content ID is detection, not a licence; have a music-free alternative if a claim lands.

## Privacy, consent, sensitivity
- The couple's contract is not guest consent. Separate likeness release for the couple, an opt-out for marketing, guardian permission before featuring children in promotion, no naming or geotagging children.
- Teasers, portfolios and thumbnails exclude distress, intoxication, conflict, private letters and vulnerable footage unless the couple approves; honour removal requests promptly.
- Audio capture can trigger recording-consent law that differs by US state and country; use visible recording, notice and a conservative approach, and ask a local lawyer.
- Sharing transcripts or footage with third-party AI or cloud tools: send only what the client's terms allow.

## Delivery and aftercare
1. Password-protected gallery with streaming and original-quality download; stated gallery duration; USB only as a keepsake, never the sole copy; master kept internally.
2. Teaser exported separately in 9:16 with captions; platform specs and caption export: `resolve-delivery-specs`, `resolve-subtitles`. WCAG 2.2 expects captions with speaker IDs and meaningful sound; auto-captions are a draft.
3. QC the exported file end to end and confirm every contracted deliverable is present (`resolve-qc-gate`).
4. Archive: 3-2-1 backups (three copies, two media, one off-site), encrypted, restore tested; tell the client the retention schedule before booking (published examples: raw 6–12 months, masters and projects 3–7 years, gallery 12 months) and never promise indefinite storage.

## Mistakes
Assuming "royalty-free" or a venue licence clears sync · treating the couple's signature as guest consent · promising shots a venue forbids · no revision limits · teaser with a crying child or family conflict · delivering only a USB · deleting raw footage without notice · unproofed auto-captions.
