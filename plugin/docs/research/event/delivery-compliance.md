# Event video delivery and compliance — research digest

Date: 2026-11-22. Source: one `deep` You.com run (34 cited sources: location-agreement guides, sponsorship and brand guidance, GDPR/ICO, NSPCC, WCAG, ADA summaries, PRO licensing pages, platform help pages, venue labour notes).
**Operational guidance, not legal advice**; law, collective agreements, venue contracts, sponsor agreements and platform rules control. Platform numbers are as reported on 2026-11-22 and change.

## Clearance register (per shot and deliverable)

People: speaker/performer release, attendee notice or consent basis, minor/guardian consent. Location: venue agreement, permission, permits, insurance, restricted areas. Brands: sponsor agreement, approved logo files, guidelines, approval rights. Music: live, background, sync, master, mechanical, platform rights as applicable.
Presentations: speaker licence plus third-party image/video/chart/quote/font/music permissions. Deliverable: owner, permitted edits, territory, term, media, monetisation, captions, takedown. Archive: retention, access, deletion, legal hold. A permit (public-authority rules) and a location agreement (property owner's access and appearance rights) are different; both may be needed.

## Sponsors

Written sponsorship/IP licence covering: approved logo version and clear space, exact placement (stage screen, lower thirds, holding slides, end card, social cutdowns), minimum screen time and prominence, equal treatment, verbal mentions, tags and hashtags, booth footage, exclusivity, approvals, dates and review deadlines, make-goods, reporting,
ownership and licence of the final video, term, territory, media, paid-ad rights, takedown. Use the unmodified approved logo; do not imply endorsement beyond the agreement; if a logo cannot be cleared use a text acknowledgement. Paid sponsorship needs clear disclosure per platform/country rules.

## Releases and notice

Speakers/performers: signed before recording; authorises recording voice/image/name, slides and demos, editing/excerpting/subtitling/translating/clips, publication channels, term, territory, exclusivity, compensation, bio/headshot/quote use, takedown and correction, warranty on third-party material. Recording permission is not publication or derivative permission; presenters keep rights in deck and performance unless assigned or licensed.
Union talent: identify the agreement and signatory status first; collective agreements control (minimums, residuals, reuse limits, digital-replica consent).
Attendees: registration terms, entrance and room notices, verbal announcement, an opt-out route (no-camera zone, lanyard colour, seating), crew opt-out list, blur/crop/remove. A notice is transparency, not necessarily consent. GDPR: ordinary documentation may sometimes rest on legitimate interests after a documented assessment, close-ups/portraits/advertising may need consent; lawful basis and image law vary.
Notice text should name the organiser, purposes, how to avoid filming, and link to a full privacy notice (purposes, lawful basis, recipients, transfers, retention, rights, complaints; GDPR Art. 13). Crew rules: no registration data, badges, medical info, private conversations or personal-data screens, no toilets/changing/security rooms, no publication outside the approved list.

## Minors

Child-appropriate assent plus written parent/carer consent (age and mechanism vary; NSPCC suggests parental consent under 16, purpose and use explained, retention stated). Separate releases for internal, public, social and paid use; no names, school or location metadata unless necessary; avoid close-ups without consent; no-camera zones; secure storage; do not livestream children unless assessed and authorised; flag safeguarding concerns and court orders. A crowd shot is lower risk but not automatically publishable.

## Venue and labour

Confirm who can grant rights, permitted rooms and hours, crew size, camera positions, cabling/fire-safety/rigging/drone/noise rules, signage and competing-brand limits, insurance and indemnities, AV exclusivity and house feeds, recording fees, final-edit approval, and union jurisdiction (convention venues may require union labour or venue-approved crews). A booked meeting room is not permission to film lobbies and corridors.

## Music

Live music in venue and DJ/background: public-performance licence (often venue/organiser). Music fixed to video: sync (composition) plus master (recording). Live performance recorded for publication: composition/performance permission plus recording rights. Livestream or self-hosted video: digital public-performance plus sync/master. Audio release: possibly mechanical.
A venue's PRO licence does not clear sync. Clear both publisher and label for commercial recordings. "Royalty-free" is a licence with terms: check paid media, territory, term, platforms, edits, sublicensing to clients and sponsors; keep invoice and licence. No "30-second rule". If unavailable: mute/replace, commission with written assignment or licence, or use a library whose licence covers the deliverables.

## Slides and third-party content

Collect decks in advance, speaker warrants rights to images, charts, quotes, video, music, fonts, demos, logos, case-study and customer data. Internet availability, attribution, educational use or brevity does not authorise recording or publishing. Record only the cleared deck, allow "do not record" slides or replacements, crop/replace uncleared slides, separate permission for PDFs, transcripts, screenshots and social clips, keep records. Do not assume fair use or fair dealing.

## Captions and accessibility

Operationally target WCAG 2.2 AA unless law or contract says otherwise: captions for prerecorded media (1.2.2, A), live captions (1.2.4, AA), speaker ID and meaningful sound, transcript or media alternative where required, audio description for visual information not in audio (1.2.5, AA), accessible player, captions for sponsor slides, lower thirds and audience questions. Auto-captions are not automatically compliant.
US: ADA Title II (state/local government), Section 508 (federal), Title III effective communication, FCC rules for some TV-originated online programming are not interchangeable; private web video has no single detailed Title III technical rule, WCAG is a benchmark.

## Platform notes (as reported 2026-11-22; verify)

YouTube: MP4/H.264, progressive, source frame rate, AAC-LC/Opus 48 kHz; published bitrates 8 Mbps for 1080p SDR at 24–30 fps, 35–45 Mbps for 4K at 24–30 fps. LinkedIn: MP4/MOV, 256×144 to 4096×2304, aspect 1:2.4 to 2.4:1, up to 5 GB, generally up to 10 minutes for company-page publishing. Instagram: separate 9:16, typically 1080×1920, limits differ by surface. Vimeo: high-quality MP4/H.264 master with caption and transcript files; storage depends on plan.
Deliver a master, platform encodes, caption file, transcript, thumbnail/stills, stems if contracted, metadata, music cue sheet, rights and consent report.

## Archive, privacy, go/no-go

Separate master archive (final files, captions, transcript, project, rights records), access copy, raw footage (only while needed), consent/rights register. GDPR gives no universal retention period; justify, review, erase or anonymise. Encrypted storage and transfer, role-based access, MFA, audit logs, vendor DPAs, deletion that reaches cloud copies, caches, exports and backups. For EU/UK-linked events identify controllers and processors, document lawful basis per use, provide notice at capture, minimise identifiable people, honour objection and withdrawal, assess transfers, no facial recognition without a separate assessment; livestreaming is processing.
Go/no-go before publishing: venue permission and permit record, sponsor approvals, speaker/performer/minor releases, attendee notice and opt-out log, music/slide/third-party clearances, union and crew confirmation, caption QA record, platform export and accessibility check, retention/takedown/incident owner.
