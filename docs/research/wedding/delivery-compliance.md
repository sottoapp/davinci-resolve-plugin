# Wedding delivery, licensing and client care — research digest

Date: 2026-11-22. Source: one `deep` You.com run (33 cited sources: wedding-business guides, library licence pages, platform music terms, COPPA/FTC, recording-consent summaries, venue policies, W3C WCAG, CISA).
**Operational guidance, not legal advice.** Turnaround, retention and revision figures are what published studios commonly state, not standards. Licence and platform terms change; each skill says to verify.

## Package and turnaround (as published)

Baseline: one highlight (about 3–8 min), full ceremony, full or separate speeches/reception, social teaser (30–90 s, often vertical), private streaming/download gallery, captions on request or standard,
music-licence documentation and a usage/privacy status. Turnaround commonly 6–12 weeks, peak season longer; teaser first, roughly 1–2 weeks. Contract states when the clock starts, separate deadlines per deliverable,
peak extensions, client-feedback deadlines, consequence of missed estimates, rush availability and price.

Revisions: one or two consolidated rounds, timestamped feedback in one message; define minor (trims, sequencing, title fixes, audio level, colour tweaks) vs major re-edit (new storyline, substantial replacement, new
deliverable, post-approval re-edit); music replacement; response time per round; extra-round and rush fees; final approval / deemed-acceptance.

## Delivery stack

Password-protected gallery with streaming and original-quality download; private streaming link (unlisted/password/downloadable/renewable stated); optional USB, never the sole copy; H.264/H.265 MP4 for delivery with a
higher-quality master kept internally; separate 9:16 teaser; WebVTT/SRT or a captioned export. State gallery duration explicitly (for example 12 months); never call a platform "permanent" unless its terms say so.

## Music

Putting music on wedding footage normally needs a **sync licence** (composition) and a **master-use licence** (recording). A venue's or DJ's public-performance licence does not grant either. Buying, streaming, crediting,
using a short excerpt or keeping the film "private" does not create sync permission. Libraries (reported): Musicbed (sync and master; wedding single-song licence is for the couple's film, not business promotion), Artlist
(Pro covers client work; Social covers only the creator's own channels, not client work; the music asset cannot be transferred for independent reuse), Soundstripe (Pro for client projects; post-cancellation, cleared
projects stay covered; client-channel uploads may need a clearance code). Rule: store invoice, licence certificate, track, plan, download date, project, platforms and licence version with the project.
Platforms: Meta prohibits commercial/non-personal use of unlicensed music; TikTok General Music Library is personal non-commercial, Commercial Music Library sounds generally limited to TikTok; YouTube Content ID is detection not
licence, a valid licence can still draw a claim needing a dispute or clearance code; a track cleared inside one app is not cleared for the downloaded master elsewhere. Contract: who selects and pays, which versions carry licensed
music, who handles later uploads, music-free alternative if a claim occurs.

## Privacy and consent

Couple's contract is not guest consent; separate likeness release for the couple; consent for identifiable guests in promotional use; "no marketing use" option recorded in the project file. Minors: guardian permission before
promotional use, no naming/tagging/geolocating, honour removal requests. COPPA concerns online services directed to children or knowingly collecting data from under-13s and is not a blanket wedding rule; state privacy, publicity and
child-protection law may apply. Audio recording consent: US states differ (one-party vs all-party); use visible recording and notice, conservative all-party workflow across states, local legal advice. No recording in bathrooms or
changing rooms; pause for private, medical or confidential conversations unless lawfully requested.

Venues and officiants: written policies first (camera positions, aisle movement, flash/LED/drone, wireless audio, insurance, officiant approval of microphones, third-party publication). Their rules override the couple's wishes;
the contract makes them a disclosed limitation.

## Copyright and contract clauses (US framing)

Copyright arises on fixation, belongs to the author; commissioned work is work-for-hire only with statutory conditions and a signed writing; transfer needs a signed writing. Separate clauses: ownership of raw footage, project files,
audio and final edits; client licence (term, territory, personal viewing/sharing/posting); restrictions (resale, sublicensing, commercial use, re-editing, music extraction, vendor advertising); studio portfolio licence with opt-out
or privacy fee; guest/minor releases; raw footage terms; music policy; venue/officiant limits; deliverables, formats, deadlines, fees; data security and deletion schedule; liability and force majeure; governing law reviewed locally.

## Backup and archive

CISA 3-2-1: three copies, two media types, one off-site; encrypt, keep an offline copy, test restores. Ingest to two independent drives, no card formatting until both verified, working copy plus local backup plus encrypted
off-site, separate credentials and MFA, licences/contracts/releases/project files/masters stored with media, restrict access to raw footage. No universal retention period; a published-practice example is raw 6–12 months after
delivery, project files and masters 3–7 years, gallery 12 months unless extended, contracts/releases/licences longer. Publish the policy before booking, remind before deletion, sell archive extension, never promise indefinite retention.

## Captioning

WCAG 2.2 requires captions for prerecorded synchronized media at Level A, including speaker identification and meaningful non-speech sound (laughter, applause, music). Deliver human-reviewed captions, VTT/SRT, a captioned social
export (platforms do not reliably keep caption files), a transcript for ceremony and speeches, audio description or a descriptive transcript where visuals matter. Auto-captions are a draft. WCAG is a technical standard, not
itself a universal private-business law.

## Sensitive moments

Ask in planning about no-film people, family conflict, medical issues, children, private or religious rituals, surprises; keep a do-not-film list shared with every shooter and editor; do not film bathrooms, changing, medical
care, intoxication, arguments, grief, private letters or intimate conversations unless lawfully requested; no embarrassing or vulnerable footage in teasers, thumbnails or ads; couple approves teasers containing conflict, crying,
children, religious/private content or sensitive speeches; act promptly on removal requests; do not treat distress as automatically good storytelling.
